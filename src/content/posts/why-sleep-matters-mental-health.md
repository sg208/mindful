We often treat sleep as a luxury we can skip when life gets busy. But sleep is one of the most powerful tools we have for mental and emotional wellbeing. When we don't get enough quality sleep, our ability to regulate emotions drops. Small setbacks feel overwhelming. We're more likely to react with irritability or anxiety.

During sleep, your brain processes the events of the day. It sorts through experiences, strengthens important memories, and softens the emotional charge of difficult moments. Skimping on sleep means that processing never fully happens. Feelings can feel raw or stuck. Research has shown that even one or two nights of poor sleep can worsen mood and make it harder to cope with stress.

Sleep also affects the way we relate to others. When we're tired, we're more likely to snap at loved ones, misread social cues, or withdraw. Prioritizing sleep isn't selfish—it helps us show up more calmly and clearly for the people who matter to us. Aim for seven to nine hours of sleep most nights. Keep a consistent schedule, even on weekends. Limit screens an hour before bed and create a calm wind-down routine.
