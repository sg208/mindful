Your body and brain benefit from signals that it's time to slow down. A bedtime routine doesn't have to be long—even 20 to 30 minutes can make a difference. The key is consistency. Doing similar things in a similar order each night helps your brain associate those steps with sleep.

Dim the lights and put screens away at least an hour before bed. Blue light from phones and laptops can interfere with melatonin. Instead, try reading something light, gentle stretching, or listening to calm music. Some people find a warm shower or a cup of caffeine-free tea helpful.

If your mind races when you lie down, try writing a short list of tomorrow's tasks on paper. Keep the bedroom cool, dark, and quiet. Reserve the bed for sleep and intimacy. Over time, your routine becomes a cue that sleep is coming. If you don't fall asleep within 20 minutes, get up and do something calm until you feel sleepy again.
