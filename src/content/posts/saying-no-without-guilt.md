Saying no is a form of self-care. Every time you say yes when you mean no, you're giving away time and energy you might not have. That can lead to burnout, resentment, and worse mental health. Learning to decline kindly is a skill worth building. It doesn't make you selfish; it makes you sustainable.

You don't owe a long explanation. "I'm not able to take that on right now" or "That doesn't work for me" is enough. The other person might be disappointed; that's okay. You're not responsible for managing everyone's feelings. You're allowed to have limits even when others wish you didn't.

It can help to pause before answering. "Let me check my schedule and get back to you" buys you time to decide what you actually want. Practice in low-stakes situations first. The more you say no when it's true for you, the easier it gets. The people who respect your boundaries are the ones worth keeping close.
