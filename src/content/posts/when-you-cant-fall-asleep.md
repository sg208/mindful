Struggling to fall asleep can make the next day harder and add anxiety about sleep itself. If you've been in bed for 20 minutes and you're still awake, get up. Do something calm and dimly lit until you feel sleepy, then return to bed. Lying there for hours trains your brain to associate the bed with wakefulness. The "20-minute rule" is a core part of cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia.

Keep a consistent wake time, even on weekends. Avoid caffeine after midday and limit alcohol. Make the bedroom for sleep (and intimacy) only; work and screens elsewhere when possible. Your brain should learn: this room means rest.

If your mind won't quiet down, try a boring mental task: count backward from 300 by threes. Some people find that a "worry dump" earlier in the evening helps: write down everything on your mind and tell yourself you've parked it until tomorrow. If insomnia persists, a healthcare provider can rule out sleep disorders and suggest next steps.
