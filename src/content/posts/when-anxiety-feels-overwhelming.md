When anxiety spikes, it can feel like the world is closing in. Your heart races, your thoughts spiral, and it's hard to think clearly. In those moments, the goal isn't to fix everything—it's to bring your nervous system back to a slightly calmer state so you can cope. You're not failing; you're having a normal human response to perceived threat.

Grounding helps. Name five things you can see, four you can touch, three you can hear, two you can smell, one you can taste. It pulls your attention into the present and away from the worst-case story in your head. Breathing slowly—in for four counts, out for six—can dial down the fight-or-flight response. The extended exhale is especially powerful.

If you're alone, try holding something cold, like an ice cube or a cold drink. The sensation can interrupt the anxiety loop. Some people find that naming the emotion out loud reduces its grip. Remind yourself that this feeling will pass; it always has before. If anxiety is frequent or severe, reach out to a therapist or doctor. Having a few go-to strategies gives you a sense of agency when the wave hits.
