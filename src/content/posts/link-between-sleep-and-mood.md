Sleep and mental health are deeply connected. When we don't sleep well, we're more vulnerable to low mood, irritability, and anxiety. When we're depressed or anxious, sleep often suffers. It can feel like a trap. Poor sleep worsens mood; low mood disrupts sleep. The good news is that improving one often helps the other.

Stick to a regular sleep schedule. Get daylight during the day and limit it at night. Move your body; exercise can improve both sleep and mood, even if it's just a short walk. If racing thoughts keep you up, try writing them down before bed or using a simple breathing exercise.

Be patient with yourself. If you've had a bad night, try not to catastrophize. One night doesn't define you. If you've made these changes and still struggle, talk to a doctor or therapist. Sleep disorders and mood disorders often need professional support. You deserve support for both.
