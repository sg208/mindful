Your nervous system has a "gas pedal" (sympathetic) and a "brake" (parasympathetic). When you're stressed or anxious, the gas pedal is pressed. The good news is you can actively engage the brake. Slow, extended exhales do this—try breathing out longer than you breathe in. Cold exposure (splashing your face, holding ice) can also trigger a calming response.

Physical grounding works for many people: feel your feet on the floor, press your hands into your legs, or notice the weight of your body in the chair. Movement can help too: a brisk walk or shaking out your limbs can discharge pent-up tension. So can weighted blankets, a warm drink, or wrapping yourself in a cozy blanket—comfort signals safety.

These aren't a substitute for addressing the sources of stress or getting professional help when needed. But in the moment, they can create a bit of space so you can think and choose instead of reacting from overwhelm. Keep a short list of what works for you—two or three go-to strategies.
