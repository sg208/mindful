Something goes sideways—a conversation that landed wrong, a day you meant to handle better, another night of lying awake with the list. And before you know it, there is a voice cataloguing every misstep, every thing you should have done differently. That voice can feel like responsibility. It rarely feels like relief.

Self-compassion is not about lowering the bar or excusing what hurt. It is about how you hold yourself while you figure out what to do next. There is a part of you that already knows how to be steady with someone who is struggling—the part that finds the right words for a friend at 2 a.m. That same quiet knowing is available to you, for you. Inner work often begins right there: noticing whose voice is running the room.

When self-criticism gets loud, you do not have to argue with it. Try pausing just long enough to feel your feet on the floor. Then offer yourself one grounded sentence—not a fix, just a truth: _This is a hard stretch, and I am still in it._ If it helps, rest a hand on your chest and breathe once before the mind picks the thought back up. Common humanity is real; the weight you carry today is a weight others know too.

You do not have to become someone who never doubts. You only need to keep returning to a tone that does not leave you smaller. That return, again and again, is the practice.
