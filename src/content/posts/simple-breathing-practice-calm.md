Breathing exercises are one of the fastest ways to shift your nervous system from "alert" to "calm." You don't need special equipment or a lot of time. Try this: breathe in slowly for four counts, hold for a moment if it feels okay, then breathe out slowly for six counts. Repeat for a few minutes. The longer exhale activates the part of your nervous system that helps you rest and digest.

You can do this at your desk, in the car (parked), or before bed. If counting feels distracting, just focus on making the out-breath longer than the in-breath. When your mind wanders, gently bring it back. The ratio matters more than the exact number. The goal is to extend the exhale relative to the inhale.

Some people find it helpful to place a hand on the belly and feel it rise and fall. You might also pair the breath with a simple phrase: breathing in, "I am here"; breathing out, "I am calm." This isn't a replacement for professional help, but as a daily tool it can create a little more space between a stressor and your reaction. Your breath is always available.
