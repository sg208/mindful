Self-care has become a buzzword, but it doesn't have to mean expensive routines or hours of free time. The most effective self-care is often small, consistent, and woven into your existing day. A few minutes of stillness, a short walk, or a deliberate pause before reacting can shift how you feel. The key is consistency, not intensity. A little bit, often, beats a rare splurge.

Start with one thing: maybe five minutes of quiet with your eyes closed, or a single stretch when you wake up. The goal isn't to add more tasks to your list but to create moments that remind you that you matter. When we're stressed, we often put ourselves last. Small acts of self-care signal to your nervous system that you're worth pausing for.

Consider what actually restores you. For some people it's movement; for others it's stillness. There's no single right way. The only requirement is that it feels replenishing rather than draining. Choose one or two practices that feel doable and stick with them. Over time, they become habits that support your mental health without feeling like extra work.
