Stress is a normal response to demand and change. But when it's constant and we don't recover, it can affect our mood, sleep, and health. The first step is noticing. Are you irritable, tired, or catching every cold? Do you feel on edge or find it hard to relax? Stress isn't just in your head—it shows up in your body and behavior.

You might not be able to remove the stressor, but you can add buffers. Move your body in a way that feels good. Connect with someone who gets you. Protect your sleep. Even small breaks during the day can help: a short walk, a few deep breaths, or stepping away from the screen. Build those micro-rests into your day so stress doesn't compound.

Look at what you can control. Sometimes we accept situations as fixed when there are small changes we could make. Not everything is changeable, but we often have more agency than we think. If stress is overwhelming or affecting your daily life, talking to a therapist or doctor can help. You deserve to feel more than just "getting by."
